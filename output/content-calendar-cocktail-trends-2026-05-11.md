## How to Use This Calendar

- **TikTok** posts = video-first, visual moment required (pour, color change, smoke, reveal)
- **Instagram** posts = Reels or carousel; carousels work well for tutorials and breakdowns
- One post per week minimum. Two is better. Quality over quantity.
- Film in batches — one session can produce 3–4 posts

## Evergreen Content Ideas (use anytime)

These don't expire — post when you have a gap or need to fill the week:

- **"What I actually stock behind my bar vs. what's on the menu"** — TikTok, candid
- **Ingredient spotlight series** — one unusual ingredient per post, what it tastes like, how to use it
- **"The drink I wish more people ordered"** — VinePair reported 13 bartenders answered this; do your own version
- **Speed build with no voiceover** — just the sounds: ice, shaker, pour, garnish
- **Reaction to a viral TikTok drink** — make it yourself, rate it honestly

---

## Posting Rules

1. **Film the moment, not the drink.** The pour, the smoke lift, the color change — that's what gets shared.
2. **First 2 seconds decide everything.** Start mid-action on TikTok. No intros.
3. **Carousels for depth, Reels for reach.** Use carousels when you have something to teach. Use Reels when you want new eyes.
4. **One strong hook per caption.** Don't describe the drink — say something that makes someone stop. "This is why your Cosmo tastes wrong." "The $3 ingredient changing cocktail menus globally."
5. **Batch your filming.** Set up the bar once, shoot 3–4 drinks in a row. One session = one month of content.
